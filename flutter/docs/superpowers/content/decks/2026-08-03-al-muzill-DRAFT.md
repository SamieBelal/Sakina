## 2 · Why this deck runs on register, not just sourcing

The task that assigned this pair states the risk plainly, and it is correct: **a deck about Allah
humiliating someone, met at night by someone already feeling humiliated, is exactly what bar 5
exists to prevent.** This deck answers it the way `al-qabid@1` answered a structurally similar
problem for withholding — **structurally, in what the text itself does, not by reassurance authored
onto the beats.** No beat here says "this isn't about you," "you'll be fine," or "it's for a reason."
The bridge names a third party ("someone," not "you"); every story beat is about an idol and a
community's collective act, never a named individual singled out for shame; and the takeaway's claim
is a fact about the text's own structure (§7), not a promise about the reader's outcome.

## 7 · Can Al-Muzill stand alone? — my explicit read

**No — not honestly, on the evidence this draft collected.** This is stated as a finding, not a
hedge, and the reasoning is textual, not a feeling about the Name's difficulty.

**The fact that decides it.** §3's enumeration is exhaustive over the full Qur'ān: **`dh-l-l` in the
sense this Name means — Allah humiliating a person — occurs exactly once, at 3:26, and it is
coordinated by a single `وَ` with the clause that demonstrates Al-Muizz's act.** This is not a case
of "the two Names are thematically close" or "the duʿā happens to pair them" (which is true of many
duplicate-`dua_arabic` groups on the ledger and does not, by itself, force a pairing — most of those
30 Names are listed `BLOCKED` on the duʿā axis without anyone concluding they can never ship alone).
**This is a case where the Name's *entire* textual demonstration, at the level of Allah's own words,
does not exist anywhere except inside the sentence that also demonstrates its twin's act.** A
standalone `al-muzill@1` would face a choice with no good branch:

1. **Trade away bar 4 entirely** and build the verse beat on 7:152 alone (`j-z-y`, not `dh-l-l`) —
   an honest deck, but a materially weaker one: the Name's own root would never reach a screen, and
   the deck's only demonstration of Allah's *finite* act would be a different verb doing adjacent
   work, not the Name's own word.
2. **Quote 3:26's humiliation clause in isolation**, with no textual signal that it is one half of a
   single coordinated sentence — which is precisely the shape the task warns against: the reader
   meets a bare statement that Allah humiliates whom He wills, with the honoring clause that sits in
   the same breath either cut silently or never shown at all.
3. **What this draft actually does**, which requires the twin: quote only "You humble whom You
   will," disclose in the takeaway that the Name never appears without its counterpart (a true,
   checkable fact about the text — see §3), and let the sibling deck carry the other half so a user
   who meets both sees the whole sentence across two visits rather than either deck asserting it
   alone.

**Comparable precedent, and why this case is stronger, not weaker.** Ad-Darr (95) was ruled
must-always-pair with An-Nāfiʿ by founder decision. Al-Qābiḍ/Al-Bāsiṭ reached "pair only" from the
text itself — but even there, `q-b-ḍ` has **two** unspent finite-verb candidates with Allah as
subject (2:245, 25:46), and in both, the expanding root sits in the *same clause*, not merely the
same sentence-family. Here, `dh-l-l` has **one** candidate, full stop, and it is not merely
accompanied by `ʿ-z-z` — it is grammatically inseparable from it in the one sentence that carries
either. **If Al-Qābiḍ's own drafter concluded "pair only" from two candidates that happen to co-occur,
the honest conclusion from one candidate that cannot occur without its opposite is at least as
strong.**

**What I am not claiming.** I am not claiming Al-Muzill is theologically illegitimate or that the
concept cannot be taught — the ḥadīth and duʿā corpus outside this specific "finite verb, Allah as
subject, in-Qur'ān" search may hold material a wider, non-Qur'ān-first sweep would find (§8's stated
limit). I am claiming that **on the evidence this pipeline's own method produces**, an honest
solo deck is either weaker (bar 4 traded away) or riskier (the humiliation clause shown without its
textual counterpart) than the paired version drafted here. **A reasoned "half of a pair only" is the
recommendation**, not a refusal to draft — both decks are drafted, complete, and ready for review;
the recommendation is about how they should be *gated for release* (both-or-neither), not about
whether either should exist.
