## 1 · The shared duʿā is **not** Qurʾānic. Do not cite it.

All four render, byte-for-byte:

> `اللَّهُمَّ احْكُمْ بَيْنَنَا وَبَيْنَ قَوْمِنَا بِالْحَقِّ وَأَنتَ خَيْرُ الْحَاكِمِينَ`
> *"O Allah, judge between us and our people in truth — You are the best of judges."*

**It reads as a verbatim quotation and is a composite.** Fetched:

| fragment | nearest āyah | what differs |
|---|---|---|
| `ٱفْتَحْ بَيْنَنَا وَبَيْنَ قَوْمِنَا بِٱلْحَقِّ` | **7:89** `رَبَّنَا ٱفْتَحْ بَيْنَنَا وَبَيْنَ قَوْمِنَا بِٱلْحَقِّ وَأَنتَ خَيْرُ ٱلْفَـٰتِحِينَ` | verb **`ٱفْتَحْ` → `احْكُمْ`**, and **`ٱلْفَـٰتِحِينَ` → `ٱلْحَـٰكِمِينَ`** — the root is swapped from `ف-ت-ح` to `ح-ك-م` |
| `خَيْرُ ٱلْحَـٰكِمِينَ` | **7:87 · 10:109 · 12:80**, all `وَ**هُوَ** خَيْرُ ٱلْحَـٰكِمِينَ` | third person → **second** person |
| `اللَّهُمَّ` | none | prefixed |

**No āyah contains this sentence.** 7:89 is also reported human speech (Shuʿayb's followers), bottom rung of §9bk.

**Ruling for all four decks: the duʿā beat carries `source: ""`.** It is an authored supplication, like `al-qabid@1`, `al-basit@1`, `al-haleem@1` and `al-baseer@1`, all of which ship with an empty `source`. Reported to the catalogue, **not actioned** (§ never rule on your own catalogue recommendation). Full rule: **§9cf**.

**Second consequence, and it is the reason this section is first:** because the duʿā beat renders identically on all four decks, **a user collecting all four sees the same duʿā four times.** That is the catalogue's construction, is unavoidable, and is disclosed rather than concealed. **No beat on any of the four claims the duʿā is Name-specific**, and each deck's engine is carried on its **takeaway**, which is fixed.

## 2 · The bar-5 ruling this group needs, made explicitly rather than assumed

**Every strong carrier for these four Names sits in a Judgment context.** That is not an accident of selection — it is what the Names *are*: The Judge, The Just, The Reckoner, The Equitable. A justice Name whose scripture avoided the settling of accounts would not be demonstrating the Name.

Bar 5 forbids *"no rebuke passages, no Fire/Judgment adjacency, no accusation of the reader."* **Read mechanically, that rejects all four Names as undraftable.** So the reason behind the bar has to be engaged rather than the wording applied:

> The bar exists because this renders **at 11pm to someone in distress**. Its purpose is register — do not frighten, do not accuse.

**For a reader who has been wronged, "no soul will be treated unjustly at all" is not a threat. It is the consolation.** The catalogue says so itself, in all four `lesson` fields, which were written independently of these decks:

- 47 — *"When the world is unjust, remember that Al-Hakam will settle every account."*
- 48 — *"Al-Adl will never wrong you — not by the weight of an atom."*
- 55 — *"Al-Haseeb counts every kindness. Nothing good is ever lost."*
- 90 — *"Al-Muqsit will balance every scale. Justice will come."*

**So the ruling is: Judgment as the reader's vindication is in register; Judgment as the reader's peril is not.** Concretely, and this is enforced beat-by-beat in each draft:

1. **No beat renders punishment of anyone**, and no beat renders `عَذَاب`, the Fire, or `ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا۟`.
2. **The reader is never the defendant.** Every rendered clause positions the reader as the one *owed* an accounting, never the one facing one.
3. **Where the carrier's neighbour turns to punishment, the beat stops at the āyah boundary** and the successor is disclosed in full, not summarised.

**This is the single most attackable decision across all four decks and it is deliberately concentrated here.** A verifier who rejects it rejects all four at once, which is the correct granularity — it should not be re-argued four times in four places.

| 48 Al-Adl | `Edl` | **28 occurrences, 2 forms** — 14× form I verb `ʿadala`, 14× noun `ʿadl`; 24 distinct āyāt | **most are commands to humans** to be just, or `ʿadala` in the sense of *"to ascribe equals"* — a different and negative sense |
| 55 Al-Haseeb | `Hsb` | **109 occurrences, 8 forms**; 102 distinct āyāt | dominated by `ḥisāb` (39×, "the reckoning") and form I `ḥasiba` (44×, *"they think / they reckon"* — human supposition). **The Name-form `ḥasīb` is only 4 occurrences** |
| 90 Al-Muqsit | `qsT` | **25 occurrences, 5 forms**; 22 distinct āyāt | `qisṭ` noun 15×; the Name-form `muqsiṭīn` **3×** — and all three are *"Allah loves those who act justly"*, i.e. **humans as subject** |

**The finding that shapes the whole group: for three of the four, the Name-form itself almost never has Allah as its grammatical subject.**

- `ٱلْمُقْسِطِينَ` (5:42, 49:9, 60:8) describes **people Allah loves**, not Allah. **Bar 1 cannot be built on the Name-form for Al-Muqsit.**
- `حَسِيبًا` (4:6, 4:86, 33:39) is a predicate epithet — *"Allah is ever an Accountant"* — which **labels rather than demonstrates**.
- `حَكَمًا` at 6:114 is inside `قُلْ`-instructed recitation — **explicitly does not carry bar 1** (§9bk).

**So three of the four decks must find a finite verb or a first-person divine act elsewhere in the root, and one (Al-Adl) must trade bar 4.** That is what the partition below does.

## 4 · The partition — which text belongs to which Name, and what each leaves

**Two decks divide one āyah (21:47) and render disjoint clauses.** This is the `al-malik@1` / `al-muizz@1` / `al-muzill@1` precedent on 3:26 — three shipped decks divide one āyah three ways, each rendering only its own clause. **What disqualifies is rendering the neighbour's clause**, which is checked below, not inferred.

**21:47 in full, fetched:**

> `وَنَضَعُ ٱلْمَوَٰزِينَ ٱلْقِسْطَ لِيَوْمِ ٱلْقِيَـٰمَةِ فَلَا تُظْلَمُ نَفْسٌ شَيْـًٔا ۖ وَإِن كَانَ مِثْقَالَ حَبَّةٍ مِّنْ خَرْدَلٍ أَتَيْنَا بِهَا ۗ وَكَفَىٰ بِنَا حَـٰسِبِينَ`
> *"And We place the scales of justice for the Day of Resurrection, so no soul will be treated unjustly at all. And if there is [even] the weight of a mustard seed, We will bring it forth. And sufficient are We as accountant."*

**One āyah, Allah's own first-person plural throughout (`نَضَعُ`, `أَتَيْنَا`, `بِنَا`), carrying three of the four Names' roots**: `قسط` (Al-Muqsit), `حسب` (Al-Haseeb), and the `ظلم`-negation that is Al-Adl's whole meaning.

| Name | renders | bar 1 | bar 4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **90 Al-Muqsit** | **21:47a** — `وَنَضَعُ ٱلْمَوَٰزِينَ ٱلْقِسْطَ … فَلَا تُظْلَمُ نَفْسٌ شَيْـًٔا` | ✅ `نَضَعُ`, 1st-pers. pl. | ✅ `ٱلْقِسْطَ` |
| **55 Al-Haseeb** | **21:47c** — `وَإِن كَانَ مِثْقَالَ حَبَّةٍ مِّنْ خَرْدَلٍ أَتَيْنَا بِهَا ۗ وَكَفَىٰ بِنَا حَـٰسِبِينَ` | ✅ `أَتَيْنَا`, 1st-pers. pl. | ✅ `حَـٰسِبِينَ` |
| **47 Al-Hakam** | **22:56** — `ٱلْمُلْكُ يَوْمَئِذٍ لِّلَّهِ يَحْكُمُ بَيْنَهُمْ ۚ فَٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ وَعَمِلُوا۟ ٱلصَّـٰلِحَـٰتِ فِى جَنَّـٰتِ ٱلنَّعِيمِ` | ✅ `يَحْكُمُ`, finite verb, Allah the subject | ✅ `يَحْكُمُ` |
| **48 Al-Adl** | **4:40** (story) + **6:115a** (root carrier) | ✅ 4:40 `إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ لَا يَظْلِمُ …` | ⚠️ **traded** — see below |

**The split of 21:47 is clean at the clause boundary.** Al-Muqsit stops at the āyah's own `ۖ` pause; Al-Haseeb begins after it. **Neither renders the other's clause** — verified by reading both drafts' beat text, not assumed from the boundary.

**Al-Adl's bar-4 trade, and why the sweep forces it.** 4:40 is the strongest text for this Name by every other measure — Allah's own voice, a demonstrated proportion (*the wrong is exact, the good is multiplied*), and a bar-5 neighbourhood that is clean on both sides — but its root is **`ظ-ل-م`, not `ع-د-ل`**. The `Edl` sweep shows why nothing better exists: of 28 occurrences, the ones with Allah as subject are `عَدَلُوا۟ بِرَبِّهِمْ` (*"they ascribe equals to their Lord"* — the **negative** sense, 6:1, 6:150, 27:60), and the rest are commands to humans (4:58, 16:90, 5:8). **6:115's `صِدْقًا وَعَدْلًا` is the only place the root describes Allah's own act positively**, so it carries bar 4 on a separate verse beat while 4:40 carries bars 1 and 2. Carrier/story split — the `al-wahid@1` and `al-muhyi@1` precedent.

## 6 · Bar 3(c) — four engines, stated together so they can be compared in one place

**This is the surface that matters for this group.** Four Names in one semantic field, one shared duʿā, two sharing an āyah. If the engines are not genuinely different, this is one deck rendered four times — and §9cd is the standing warning that no measurement detects that.

| Name | the move, in one line | the reader's position |
|---|---|---|
| **47 Al-Hakam** | **the verdict actually lands.** Not that justice exists in principle — that a day comes when it is pronounced | waiting for a case to be heard |
| **48 Al-Adl** | **the accounting is asymmetric in your favour.** The wrong is measured exactly; the good is *multiplied* | afraid of being judged fairly, and told fair is better than they feared |
| **55 Al-Haseeb** | **you do not have to make your own case.** *"Sufficient are We as accountant"* — the reckoning does not depend on you proving anything, or on anyone having seen | exhausted from being unwitnessed |
| **90 Al-Muqsit** | **the scale is set before anyone is weighed.** Equity is structural, not a favour granted case by case | suspecting the process itself is rigged |

**The four are separated by the reader's grievance, not by synonyms for justice** — *unheard* / *afraid of the verdict* / *unwitnessed* / *distrustful of the process*. **Al-Haseeb is the one at risk**, because "the reckoning is complete" is close to `al-muhsi@1`'s itemisation and to `al-ghafur@1`'s complete record (§9cd). Its separation is that **Al-Muhsi is about granularity and Al-Haseeb is about sufficiency** — one says *nothing is rounded off*, the other says *nothing more is needed from you*. Each draft carries this on its own takeaway, which is fixed and not AI-replaced.
