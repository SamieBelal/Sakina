## 2 · The root sweep — the hardest ground in this pair, stated at full strength

`corpus.quran.com/qurandictionary.jsp?q=kbr` (fetched 2026-08-03): root **ك-ب-ر occurs 161 times**
(word-occurrence count). Independently re-derived over the full 6,236-āyah Uthmānī text (same
infix-tolerant skeleton method): **150 distinct āyāt**, **158 word-occurrences** (7 āyāt carry the
root twice: 2:217 ×3, 2:219/17:21/17:111/25:21/40:35/71:7 ×2 each). **A residual gap of 3 against
corpus's 161 word-count is disclosed, not reconciled** — it does not affect this deck's citations,
since 7:13, 7:146, 10:78, 45:37 and 59:23 were each individually confirmed against corpus's
per-form breakdown (Form-V verb = 2, noun `kibriyā'` = 2), which is what this deck's selection
actually turns on.

**The Name's own exact form, `ٱلْمُتَكَبِّرُ`, occurs EXACTLY ONCE in the whole Qurʾān** — at 59:23,
inside a **nine-Name enumeration chain** (`... ٱلْعَزِيزُ ٱلْجَبَّارُ ٱلْمُتَكَبِّرُ`), the worst-case
form of "trailing epithet" named anywhere in this project: not a pair, a nine-item list, directly
preceded by `ٱلْجَبَّارُ` (Al-Jabbar, shipped). **Unusable under bar 1 on two grounds at once.**

**Corpus confirms: "Form V verb (yatakabbaru): 2 times."** Both located and read — **7:13**
(`تَتَكَبَّرَ`, Allah's direct speech to Iblīs, negating the claim *for a creature*) and **7:146**
(`يَتَكَبَّرُونَ`, a general third-person description of disbelievers). **Neither has Allah as the
one being arrogant/great.** The verb form is structurally reserved, everywhere it appears, for
creatures being denied or condemned a claim that isn't theirs — which is itself the theological
content: kibriyāʾ is categorically unavailable to anything created.

**The noun form is the clean, positive carrier.** Corpus: "Noun kib'riyā: 2 times." Both located —
**10:78** (Firʿawn's people accusing Mūsā/Hārūn of wanting *"greatness in the land"* — human speech,
an accusation, rejected on the same ground the ledger already uses for 7:196/12:101/10:62) and
**45:37** (`وَلَهُۥ ٱلْكِبْرِيَآءُ فِى ٱلسَّمَـٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ` — Allah's own third-person
narration: *"And to Him belongs [all] grandeur within the heavens and the earth"*). **45:37 is the
only clean, positive, Allah-attributed occurrence of this root anywhere in the Qurʾān outside the
unusable 59:23 chain.** No second candidate exists.

**Partition against Al-Kabeer (catalogue id 53, concurrently drafting elsewhere).** `ٱلْكَبِيرُ` (the
plain adjective — a different derived form from the Form-V `ٱلْمُتَكَبِّرُ`) occurs 40 times per
corpus, overwhelmingly in the fixed pair `ٱلْعَلِىُّ ٱلْكَبِيرُ` (22:62, 31:30, and similar). **None
of these are touched by this deck** — this Name's ground is the reflexive/intensive form ("the One
entitled to greatness"), not the plain adjective ("great/greatest"), which is a different Name's
likely territory. Stated so a future Al-Kabeer drafter finds this deck has not pre-spent 22:62/31:30.

## 7 · Register — the row a verifier will attack first, addressed directly

**My own read, stated plainly because the task requires it: this is a real, non-zero register risk —
smaller than a punishment-passage anchor (Firʿawn drowning, Thamūd's earthquake) but larger than
`al-khafid@1`'s camel race, and larger than `al-qahhar@1`'s own residual risk (§6 of that draft).**
The scene narrates a being refused and told to leave. That is not nothing.

**What keeps it inside bar 5, on my judgment:**

1. **The address is never to "you."** Every clause of the quoted verdict is grammatically
   second-person **to Iblīs** (`لَكَ`/`فِيهَا`), inside a narrated exchange with a named, non-human
   party. Beat 4 marks this with the bracket-free but unmistakable frame ("The answer came back the
   same moment") — the reader is never grammatically inside the rebuke.
2. **The harsher clause is paraphrased, not quoted.** 7:13's full text is *"…it is not for you to be
   arrogant therein. So get out; indeed, you are of the debased."* The beat quotes only the first
   clause verbatim (the root-bearing, theologically load-bearing one) and paraphrases the demotion
   ("He was told to leave") rather than rendering "debased" as a direct quotation on screen.
3. **The takeaway is written to point outward.** *"Nothing posturing as bigger than you tonight has
   ever outranked that [refusal]"* — the grammatical subject of "posturing" is external, never the
   reader's own conduct. No beat anywhere says or implies "you are arrogant," "watch your pride," or
   any variant that turns the Name's demonstration back onto the reader.
4. **I found no alternative that both demonstrates the root and avoids narrating any refusal at
   all.** The enumeration in §2 is exhaustive on this question — the story options are the 59:23 chain
   (unusable on bar 1 alone), 7:13 (this deck's choice), or a full bar-4 trade to a story that never
   touches the root. I considered and set aside two bar-4-trade alternatives (Namrūd's confounding,
   Bilqis's recognition — both below) and judge 7:13 the strongest text available, while naming the
   cost rather than hiding it.

**Considered and set aside, with reasons:**

- **Namrūd/Ibrāhīm's debate (2:258)** — non-violent, register-safer on its face (a king's claim
  answered by a fact, no punishment named), but set aside for crowding (`al-qadir@1` already holds
  2:260, four āyāt on, in the same sūrah region, with a previously-rejected candidate at 2:259 in the
  same neighbourhood) and for a weaker bar-1 carrier (the climactic line is Ibrāhīm's own reported
  argument, not Allah's direct speech).
- **Bilqis's recognition (27:22–44)** — genuinely register-safe (self-chosen, dignified, non-violent),
  but the surrounding passage is dense with *other* concurrently-claimed Names' vocabulary (27:23's
  "great throne" uses `ʿ-ẓ-m`, Al-Azeem/Al-Majeed's reserved root-pair; 27:34's "kings…abase the
  honoured" uses `ʿ-z-z`/`dh-l-l`, Al-Muizz/Al-Muzill's exact reserved clause, per
  `.context/claims/43.md`/`44.md`) — quoting near either clause risked spending concurrently-claimed
  ground. `al-kareem@1` (shipped) also already holds 27:40, four āyāt from the likely climax.
- **Sahih Muslim 2788a** (`أَيْنَ ٱلْجَبَّارُونَ أَيْنَ ٱلْمُتَكَبِّرُونَ`, "Where are the tyrants?
  Where are the arrogant?") — the most obvious ḥadīth candidate, and the one this Name's catalogue
  history (see the top of this draft) makes especially tempting to reach for. Flagged in
  `.context/claims/4.md` (Al-Malik) as rejected there on bar 3 for naming both Al-Jabbar and
  Al-Mutakabbir. Attempted independent verification via Wayback
  (`web.archive.org/web/20250730094609/https://sunnah.com/muslim:2788a`, HTTP 200, fetched
  2026-08-03) — the archived capture is a JS-rendered page and the hadith text did not resolve in
  static HTML in this pass. **Not independently verified; not used regardless of that outcome.** Set
  aside primarily on register: a Day-of-Judgment address calling out "the arrogant" by name, in a
  context of humiliation, is exactly the *"Allah is greater than you and will humble you"* framing
  the task names as the bar-5 failure mode for this Name specifically.

argued with. The bridge names that directly. The story answers it with the oldest instance available:
before anything else existed to be arrogant about, the very first claim to being "better than" someone
else was made — and refused, immediately, in the same breath it was spoken. Not weighed, not
negotiated. The verse follows in Allah's own voice: all greatness, everywhere, belongs to Him — not
"most" of it, not the impressive kind, **all of it, in the heavens and the earth.** The takeaway closes
the loop: whatever is posturing as bigger than the reader tonight has never once outranked that first
refusal.

**Does it accuse the reader? My own read, stated at the strength I actually hold it: mostly no, with a
real residual I am not going to smooth over.** The grammar never turns on the reader — every rebuke in
the story is addressed to a named, non-human party, and the takeaway's only imperative energy points
outward. But this deck, unlike `al-qahhar@1`, does narrate a being directly refused and sent away, in
Allah's own second-person speech, and I did not find a text that avoids that shape entirely while still
meeting bar 1 and bar 4 on this Name's own root. A reader who has ever felt "too much," or has been
told they were "too proud" by someone using that as a weapon, could in principle hear an echo they
weren't meant to hear. I judge the safeguards in §7 sufficient — third-person distance, the paraphrase
of the harsher clause, the outward-pointing takeaway — but I am recording this as the harder of the two
decks in this pair on the reverence question, not asserting it is fully clean, and I say so because the
task asks for exactly this and a founder should weigh it knowing the alternative story options were
weaker on other bars, not that no alternative existed at all.
