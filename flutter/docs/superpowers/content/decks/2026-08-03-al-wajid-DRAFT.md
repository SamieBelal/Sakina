## 0 · What changed, and why the refusal was wrong

The refusal enumerated `w-j-d` correctly (107 occurrences, cross-checked against
`corpus.quran.com`) and correctly found that the richest cluster — 93:6–8 — has 93:6 spent by
shipped `al-waliyy@1` and 93:8 reserved for Al-Mughni. **It then excluded 93:7 by position** ("a
third deck across three consecutive āyāt") rather than testing it as a candidate. That reasoning
does not survive comparison to a precedent already in this corpus: **Qur'an 3:26 is split three
ways across three decks** — `al-malik@1` (shipped) renders the sovereignty clauses; drafted
`al-muizz@1`/`al-muzill@1` each render only their own clause (`وَتُعِزُّ مَن تَشَآءُ` /
`وَتُذِلُّ مَن تَشَآءُ`), with the neighbour's clause elided, visibly, on both. **Three decks
inside one āyah, accepted.** Three decks across three distinct āyāt, each taking only its own
clause, is a weaker claim on the identical ground.

**The real question was never adjacency. It is whether 93:7 does something 93:6 doesn't.** On the
refusal's own account, it does: 93:6 is refuge given to an orphan (no protector); 93:7 is guidance
given to someone lost (no direction). **Different act, different lack, same finder.** Tested
properly below, with the `h-d-y` root handled exactly the way `al-malik@1` handled Muizz/Muzill's —
ellipsis, disclosed.

**Route 2 (split the bar-1 carrier from the story) turned out not to be needed as a separate
mechanism** — 93:7 itself carries `w-j-d` with Allah as subject, so bar 1 and bar 4 are met on the
same beat, without a trade. The precedent (`al-wahid@1`'s 21:21-22 story / 16:51 verse split;
`al-muhyi@1`'s two independent verse beats) is still the right model for *scene-setting without
requiring the root there too* — applied below to this deck's story beats, which do not carry
`w-j-d` and do not need to.

**Route 3 (the lost-camel ḥadīth) was checked and is closed — findings in §7, not silently
dropped.**

beat attributes waiting, wanting, or withholding to the Name. Beat 8 makes no promise about *how*
the finding resolves — it stops at the finding itself, which is the elision's entire point.
