## 2 · Why this story, duʿā-first

**The shared duʿā is `اللَّهُمَّ اجْعَلْنِي رَاضِيًا بِمَا قَسَمْتَ لِي وَبَارِكْ لِي فِيهِ`** — "O
Allah, make me pleased with what You have allotted me, and bless me in it." Read first, as the
method requires: **it carries neither Name's root.** Its own root is `q-s-m` (apportion, decree),
not `q-d-m` or `ʾ-kh-r`. It does not name either Name in the vocative (unlike `al-qabid@1`/
`al-basit@1`'s `يَا قَابِضُ يَا بَاسِطُ`) — it has no vocative at all. It is a request for
contentment with an outcome already assigned, phrased generally enough to fit any pair of opposite
Names the catalogue might have put in this slot.

**I searched for a narrated source and did not find one.** A websearch surfaced a *different*,
better-known hadith qudsī about riḍā bi'l-qaḍāʾ (contentment with decree — "if you are pleased with
what I have apportioned for you, I will ease your heart…"), flagged by Ibn Taymiyyah as *Isrāʾīliyyāt*
in the source discussing it — but its wording does not match this duʿā, and it is a *report of what
Allah says*, not a first-person petition. I am not pinning it. **UNPINNED is the correct status, and
my search was not exhaustive** — no systematic sweep of the duʿā corpora was run. Full disclosure in
§6, present verbatim in both drafts.

**So the duʿā demonstrates nothing about either root, and belongs to neither deck specifically — it
is a generic riḍā-with-qadar prayer, fitting because these two Names are, textually, an antonym pair
about the ordering of a person's portion.** That finding is why this deck goes looking for the
pair's actual textual ground elsewhere, and finds it converging on one place: Bukhārī 6398.

### The `q-d-m` enumeration — full 48-occurrence corpus, cross-checked against corpus.quran.com

Method: fetched `https://corpus.quran.com/qurandictionary.jsp?q=qdm` (root ق د م), which states the
root occurs **48 times in 8 derived forms**. Cross-checked by extracting and counting every row on
the page programmatically:

| form | count (corpus.quran.com) | count (my extraction) | Allah the finite subject of the act? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Form I (`qadima`, "to precede") | 2 | 2 (11:98, 25:23) | 25:23 is "We" (Allah), but the act is nullifying disbelievers' deeds — warning register |
| **Form II (`qaddama`, "to send forth, to offer")** | **27** | **27** — summed and verified: 2:95, 2:110, 2:223, 3:182, 4:62, 5:80, 8:51, 12:48, 18:57, 22:10, 28:47, 30:36, 36:12, 38:60, 38:61, 42:48, **49:1**, 50:28, 58:12, 58:13, 59:18, 62:7, 73:20, 75:13, 78:40, 82:5, 89:24 | **ZERO.** Every one is human — overwhelmingly "what your hands sent forth" (deeds), one imperative to spend before private counsel (58:12–13), one negative command to believers (49:1) |
| Form V (`taqaddama`, "to precede") | 2 | 2 (48:2, 74:37) | human (a person's own past sin; a person's own choice to step forward) |
| Form X (`yastaqdimu`, "to advance, to precede") | 4 | 4 (7:34, 10:49, 16:61, 34:30) | negated of **humans** — the ajal formula, shared root with the twin's `ʾ-kh-r` half; **ceded to avoid the exact collision `al-qabid@1`/`al-basit@1`'s bar-3 ruling forbids** (§3, §5) |
| Nominal `aqdamūn` | 1 | 1 | n/a — "forefathers" |
| Nominal `qadam` | 8 | 8 | n/a — literal "foot" |
| Adjective `qadīm` | 3 | not enumerated | n/a — "old" (e.g. 36:39) |
| Form X participle `mustaqdimīn` | 1 | not enumerated | — |
| **Total verb forms summed** | **35** | **27+2+2+4 = 35** | matches |

**The measurement: across every verb form of `q-d-m` in the Qur'ān, Allah is never the grammatical
subject of a positive act of advancing or bringing something forward.** This is why bar 1 for this
Name cannot rest on the Qur'ān at all, and must rest on the one ṣaḥīḥ ḥadīth that names it — see §3.

**One near-instance flagged and rejected.** 25:23, `وَقَدِمْنَا إِلَىٰ مَا عَمِلُوا۟ مِنْ عَمَلٍ
فَجَعَلْنَـٰهُ هَبَآءً مَّنثُورًا` — "And We will proceed to whatever deeds they had done and We will
render them as scattered dust" — is grammatically Allah ("We") as subject of `qadima` (Form I). **Not
taken**: the act is the nullification of disbelievers' good deeds on the Day of Judgement, warning
register, no fit for the deck's audience or the task's reverence discipline.
