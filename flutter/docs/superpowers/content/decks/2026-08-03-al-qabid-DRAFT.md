## 2 · Why this story, duʿā-first

The catalogue duʿā is `يَا قَابِضُ يَا بَاسِطُ ابْسُطْ عَلَيْنَا مِنْ رَحْمَتِكَ`. **Read first, it
settles the deck**: it addresses this Name **and its opposite in one breath**, and its only
imperative is `ابْسُطْ` — *expand*. The duʿā will not let Al-Qābiḍ stand alone.

Then the enumeration said the same thing, and that is what this deck is built on.

### The `q-b-ḍ` enumeration — full 6,236-āyah Uthmānī text, not a search API (§9ac)

Corpus built by fetching all 114 chapters from `api.quran.com/api/v4/verses/by_chapter/{n}?fields=text_uthmani`
(6,236 āyāt verified), mark-folded, then matched on the consonant skeleton. **Nine occurrences:**

| āyah | word | subject of the act | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **2:245** | `يَقْبِضُ` | **Allah**, explicit, finite verb | **TAKEN — verse beat 6** |
| 2:283 | `مَّقْبُوضَةٌ` | a pledge, in a commercial ruling | human |
| 9:67 | `وَيَقْبِضُونَ` | the hypocrites, closing their hands | human; punishment āyah |
| 20:96 | `فَقَبَضْتُ` · `قَبْضَةً` | the Sāmirī | human; a sin narrative |
| **25:46** | `قَبَضْنَـٰهُ` · `قَبْضًا` | **Allah, first person** | **TAKEN — story beat 4** |
| 39:67 | `قَبْضَتُهُۥ` | Allah — but a **noun**, `His grip` | see below |
| 67:19 | `وَيَقْبِضْنَ` | **the birds**, folding their wings | not Allah; and the āyah's holder is `ٱلرَّحْمَـٰنُ` |

> **The measurement: Allah is the subject of the act in exactly two of the nine — 2:245 and 25:46.
> This deck takes both. There is nothing left on this root for a future deck to lose.**

**39:67 is deliberately not taken.** It is the Qurʾānic parallel of the ḥadīth `al-malik@1` claims
(Bukhārī 4812), whose story beat 3 already renders *"Allah will hold the whole earth, and roll all
the heavens up in His Right Hand…"* — and `al-qayyum@1`'s ledger row records `q-b-ḍ` off-screen from
the same material. **The grasp-of-the-earth image is spent twice over; this deck goes nowhere near it.**

### And the finding that makes this a pair, not a Name

**In both places Allah is the subject of `qabḍ`, the expansion is in the text with it** —
`وَيَبْصُۜطُ` in the same clause at 2:245, and `مَدَّ ٱلظِّلَّ` one āyah earlier at 25:45.
**The Qurʾān never predicates contraction of Allah on its own.**

That is not a framing choice; it is what the two texts do, and it is the whole answer to this Name's
register problem (§7). The deck does not have to *reassure* anyone that the withholding is not the
whole story — **the two texts it quotes refuse to say it alone, in Allah's words, before the deck
says anything.**

### 4a · The 2:245 elision, stated at full strength

**The verse beat begins with a visible `…` and drops the āyah's first half.** The omitted text is
`مَّن ذَا ٱلَّذِى يُقْرِضُ ٱللَّهَ قَرْضًا حَسَنًا فَيُضَـٰعِفَهُۥ لَهُۥٓ أَضْعَافًا كَثِيرَةً` —
*"Who is it that would loan Allāh a goodly loan so He may multiply it for him many times over?"*

**What this changes, said plainly rather than buried:** in its own context, `وَٱللَّهُ يَقْبِضُ
وَيَبْصُۜطُ` is reassurance **to a person being asked to spend** — do not fear that giving will
straiten you. The clause is a general statement and stands as one, but **the deck excerpts it away
from the occasion that frames it**, and the classical reading of `qabḍ`/`basṭ` *here* is material
provision. The founder is signing a verse beat that has been lifted out of a charity appeal.

**Two things make this defensible rather than a fix-later.** The elision is **visible on the beat**,
which is the whole content of the batch-2 rule; and the omitted half is **not a reversal** — it is
the same direction (multiplication), so nothing in it would embarrass the beat if a user went and
read it. **It is disclosed, not neutralised. If the founder wants the āyah quoted in full, it costs
one beat's length and nothing else.**
