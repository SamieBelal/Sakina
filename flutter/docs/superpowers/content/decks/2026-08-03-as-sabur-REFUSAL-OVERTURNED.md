## What the error actually was

**I made one of the exact two mistakes §9bo names.** That section says two prior refusals were returned and overturned "both for the same two errors: **collapsing the bar-1 carrier and the story into one text**, and excluding the best candidate on grounds the corpus doesn't hold."

**I collapsed the bar-1 carrier and the bar-4 carrier into one requirement.** Having established that no text carries *both* — no Qurʾānic occurrence predicates `ص-ب-ر` of Allah, and the one ṣaḥīḥ ḥadīth that does is rendered by a shipped deck — I concluded the Name was undraftable. **But the brief never required one text to carry both.** It says the opposite, in two places:

- **Bar 4 is explicitly tradeable** — *"Tradeable, but only with a documented full-corpus sweep proving the trade is forced."*
- **§4 of the brief: "You may split the carrier from the story."** `al-wahid@1` and `al-muhyi@1` are the standing precedents, and this wave added `al-adl@1`, whose root appears on a different beat from its story for exactly this reason.

**So the sweep I produced was not evidence for a refusal. It was the strongest possible evidence for a trade** — the one thing bar 4 asks for before yielding. **I built the argument that unlocks the bar and then used it to close the Name.**

## Two things I got wrong on the facts, both found by re-checking

1. **I reported that the Name-form has no attestation.** It has one: **`الصبور` is present in Tirmidhī 3507's enumeration**, the last Name in the list. My first search missed it because I searched for the literal string — **and `الصَّبُور` carries diacritics between the radicals**, the identical §9bq failure I had *just documented in §1 of this very file* about `أَصْبَرُ`. **I wrote the warning and then repeated the mistake four paragraphs later.** (The attestation is graded `Daʿīf`, so it changes the bar-4 handling but not the outcome — which is luck, not diligence.)
2. **I never looked for a bar-1 carrier on a different axis.** The refusal searched the *forbearance* family — where `al-haleem@1` legitimately holds the ground — and stopped. **It never asked what As-Sabur means that Al-Haleem does not.** Once asked, the answer is immediate: Al-Haleem is **restraint**, As-Sabur is **scale**, and **32:5** (`فِى يَوْمٍ كَانَ مِقْدَارُهُۥٓ أَلْفَ سَنَةٍ مِّمَّا تَعُدُّونَ`) is free, clean on both sides, and closes on `ٱلرَّحِيمُ`. **A route the refusal never tried is not a route the refusal closed** — which is the exact standard §9bo sets and I failed to meet.

## 1 · The root is never predicated of Allah in the Qurʾān

`corpus.quran.com/qurandictionary.jsp?q=Sbr` — **HTTP 200, 49,695 bytes.** Headline, quoted: *"The triliteral root ṣād bāʾ rāʾ (ص ب ر) occurs **103 times** in the Quran, in **eight derived forms**"* — 58× form I `ṣabara`, 1× form III, 3× form VIII, 4× `ṣabbār`, 15× the noun `ṣabr`, 20× `ṣābir`, 1× `ṣābirāt`, 1× `ṣābirat`, across **93 distinct āyāt**.

**Every one of the eight forms takes a human subject.** The root appears as:

- **commands** to be patient — `وَٱصْبِرْ`, `فَٱصْبِرُوا۟`, `ٱسْتَعِينُوا۟ بِٱلصَّبْرِ` (2:45, 2:153, 3:200, 8:46, 10:109, 11:115, 16:127, 20:130, 30:60, 31:17, 40:55, 40:77, 46:35, 52:48, 70:5, 73:10, 74:7 …)
- **descriptions of patient people** — `ٱلصَّـٰبِرِينَ`, `ٱلصَّـٰبِرَٰتِ` (2:155, 2:177, 3:17, 3:142, 3:146, 8:65, 8:66, 22:35, 33:35, 39:10 …)
- **the reward of patience** — 16:96, 23:111, 25:75, 28:54, 76:12
- **Mūsā and al-Khiḍr's exchange** — 18:67–82, where the whole subject is *human* inability to be patient

**There is no form `ṣabūr`, and no occurrence anywhere in which Allah is the one described as patient.** As-Sabur is a Name of the ḥadīth enumeration (Tirmidhī's list), not a Qurʾānic epithet. **Bar 4 therefore cannot be met from the Qurʾān at all** — not traded, not approximated: the predication does not exist.

> **A note on method, because it bit during this sweep.** Grepping the rendered Arabic for the plain substring `صبر` returns **nothing** in the one text that matters (§6), because `أَصْبَرُ` is written with diacritics between every radical — `أ َ ص ْ ب َ ر ُ`. **§9bq bans substring root searches and this is a live demonstration of why**: the search failed *low*, silently, on the single occurrence the whole Name depends on. The corpus, not the grep, is the instrument.

## 2 · The one text where the root **is** predicated of Allah — and it is already spent

**Sahih al-Bukhari 7378**, Kitāb at-Tawḥīd. `sunnah.com` returned **HTTP 403** (Cloudflare interstitial — *"Enable JavaScript and cookies to continue"*), so it was fetched through the Wayback Machine, whose CDX index lists four `statuscode:200` captures. Capture read in full:

> `مَا أَحَدٌ أَصْبَرُ عَلَى أَذًى سَمِعَهُ مِنَ اللَّهِ، يَدَّعُونَ لَهُ الْوَلَدَ، ثُمَّ يُعَافِيهِمْ وَيَرْزُقُهُمْ`
>
> *"None is more patient than Allah against the harmful and annoying words He hears (from the people): They ascribe children to Him, yet He bestows upon them health and provision."*
> — Narrated Abū Mūsā al-Ashʿarī. **Ṣaḥīḥ (Bukhārī, collection-level).**

`أَصْبَرُ` — elative of `ص-ب-ر`, predicated of Allah. **This is the only such predication in the primary sources this project uses**, and it is a superb text: the offence and the continued giving in one sentence, no punishment, no accusation of the reader.

**Shipped `al-haleem@1` renders it — both halves, across two story beats, in the same English:**

| `al-haleem@1` beat | text |
|---|---|
| story 4 | *"Of the people who say it, the Prophet ﷺ said: 'None is more patient than Allah against the harmful and annoying words He hears (from the people): They ascribe children to Him…'"* |
| story 5 | *"'…yet He bestows upon them health and provision.' The words are heard. The provision does not stop."* |

**It is in production. It is not a pending draft that could be re-partitioned.**

## 3 · The catalogue gave As-Sabur the same duʿā as the deck that took its text

Ids **29 Al-Haleem** and **32 As-Sabur** share one `dua_arabic` (§9ce):

> `اللَّهُمَّ إِنِّي أَسْأَلُكَ الصَّبْرَ وَأَعُوذُ بِكَ مِنَ الْجَزَعِ`

`al-haleem@1` **ships this duʿā verbatim** as its own beat 7. So an As-Sabur deck would render:

- **the same duʿā** as `al-haleem@1`, byte-for-byte, and
- **the same ḥadīth** as `al-haleem@1`, the only root-carrying text in existence, and
- **no Qurʾānic root** at all.

**That is not a deck. It is `al-haleem@1` with a different name_intro.** Bar 3 fails on all three surfaces simultaneously — (a) the roots are identical, (b) the shared word-run against a shipped deck would be the entire duʿā plus the entire ḥadīth, and (c) the move is the same move: *forbearance is the distance between what a thing has earned and what is done about it*, which is `al-haleem@1`'s takeaway, in its own words.

## 6 · Ground left, and the ordering problem this exposes

**Everything in §1's sweep is free**, since no deck can use it — it is all human patience. `al-haleem@1` holds 35:45 and Bukhārī 7378.

**The general finding, which is worth more than this Name.** `al-haleem@1` shipped *before* anyone checked whether its scripture was the only scripture available to its duʿā-partner. **The shared-duʿā map (§9ce) was not computed until 2026-08-03, after 45 decks had shipped.** So a deck took the sole carrier of an unstarted Name it was silently paired with, and nothing in the process could have caught it.

**Rule earned: for any Name in a shared-duʿā group, check what the group's already-shipped members render before drafting — and for any deck about to ship, check whether its carrier is the only carrier for a Name it shares a duʿā with.** Ids **60 Ash-Shaheed / 46 Al-Baseer** are the other shipped-partner pair, and that one survived: `al-baseer@1` uses Bukhārī 3364 (Hājar) and 58:1, leaving 4:166's `يَشْهَدُ` free. **It survived by luck, not by process.**

## 7 · What would overturn this refusal

Stated explicitly, because two prior refusals here were overturned and neither had said what would do it:

1. **Another ṣaḥīḥ narration predicating `ص-ب-ر` of Allah**, distinct from Bukhārī 7378. **This is the live route** — §4 records that no systematic ḥadīth search was run, only one targeted fetch. A verifier with working `sunnah.com` access should try this first.
2. **A ruling that `al-haleem@1` should be re-cut** — surrendering Bukhārī 7378 to As-Sabur and re-carrying Al-Haleem on 35:45 alone, which it already renders as its verse beat. **That is a real option and it is an editorial decision above this drafter's authority**, since it modifies a shipped deck. Recorded as available, not recommended.
3. **A ruling that identical duʿā + identical ḥadīth is acceptable** where the takeaways differ, on the §9ce precedent that shared duʿās are disclosed rather than engineered around. **I think this is wrong** — §9ce covers a shared *duʿā*, not a shared carrier — but it is the argument someone will make, so it is named here.

**Recommendation: hold id 32 unshipped until route 1 is tried.** The Name is not undraftable in principle; it is undraftable *given what `al-haleem@1` already renders*, and one search could change that.
