## Deck `al-muid@1` — Al-Muid

**Why this deck exists, in one line:** it is the only story in the sources where **a person says the duʿā while on the record as not believing it**, and the narration keeps the objection and the sentence in that order. That is the deck's whole payload and it lands on plan §STEP-3 criterion 2 — it reframes the reader's own situation without moralising at them.

> Something was taken from you. There are words for that moment, and meaning them is a different thing from saying them.

**Beat 2 · name_intro** *(catalog id 68 verbatim, plus one authored gloss line — see row 5.6 and the ⚠️ English-collision box)*:
> الْمُعِيدُ — Al-Muid — The Restorer — the One who brings back what is finished

**Beats 3–5 · story — "Umm Salama":**
> 1. He taught what to say the moment something is taken: **"We belong to Allah and to Him shall we return; O Allah, reward me for my affliction and give me something better than it in exchange for it…"**
> 2. When Abu Salama died she said: **"What Muslim is better than Abu Salama whose family was the first to emigrate to the Messenger of Allah…"**
> 3. Then she said the words anyway — and in her own account, Allah gave her the Messenger of Allah ﷺ in exchange.

**Beat 6 · verse** *(excerpt, marked — the Name's own root is the operative verb, and the beat's `source` line says what the āyah is about)*:
> "And it is He who begins creation; then He repeats it, and that is [even] easier for Him." — **Qur'ān 30:27 — the Name's own verb, spoken of creation itself**

**Beat 7 · duʿā** *(catalog id 68, verbatim in full)*:
> يَا مُعِيدُ أَعِدْ إِلَيَّ مَا أَخَذْتَهُ مِنِّي أَوْ أَبْدِلْنِي خَيْرًا مِنْهُ
> *Ya Mu'id, a'id ilayya ma akhadhtahu minni aw abdilni khayran minh*
> "O Restorer, return to me what was taken or replace it with something better."

**Beat 8 · takeaway:**

**Two Names in the catalogue are glossed with the same English head-word**, and both decks print their gloss on beat 2, to the same user, in the same pack. Plan §7's schema fact says bar 3 must be judged against what renders — and what renders here is *Restorer*, twice.

**What a deck can do, and what R2 did.** The ship gate byte-locks the **duʿā** to the catalogue (`test/content/name_stories_ship_gate_test.dart` — `dua` beats are compared field-by-field against `collectible_names.json`). It does **not** byte-lock `name_intro`; it only requires non-empty `arabic` and `transliteration`. And there is shipped precedent for an authored gloss line on that beat: **`al-wakeel@1` renders *"The Trustee — the Guardian you hand your affairs to"***, labelled in its own beat as *"catalog id 35, verbatim + one authored gloss line to unify the renderings the user will meet"*. So this deck now renders:

> **"The Restorer — the One who brings back what is finished"**

The gloss is not invented: it is catalog id 68's own `meaning` field (*"The One who brings back creation after its end"*) compressed to one clause. It puts the **distinction** on the same screen as the collision — `al-jabbar@1` is *something broke and was mended*, this is *something ended*.

**What it does not do, stated plainly: it does not remove the word.** *Restorer* is still the first noun on both screens. A deck cannot fix that, because both strings are the catalogue's. **Founder decision — this is a fifth catalogue flag, and the first one that is a collision rather than a defect:** either id 9's or id 68's `english` needs to stop containing *Restorer*, or the two Names must never be adjacent in a pack. `al-jabbar@1` is already shipped, so changing id 9 means changing shipped rendered content. **This deck does not recommend which; it records that the choice exists and that no deck-level edit closes it.**

### ⚠️ The honest weakness the founder must weigh: the āyah and the story are doing two different jobs

Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim 918a is about **a life being replaced after a loss**. Qur'ān 30:27 is about **creation being re-originated**. They are joined by a single idea — *bringing back is not harder than making in the first place* — and that join is real, but it is an inference, not a quotation.

**R2 changes what the deck does about that, and the change is the one the verdict asked for.** Revision 1 wrote *"the deck asserts the join in no beat"* and defended itself on that. **That defence was false in the format's own terms**, and the verifier said so exactly: `BeatRevealFlow` is a tap-through spine, beat 5 (she was given a replacement) runs straight into beat 6 (He repeats creation) with nothing between them, and **juxtaposition on a beat spine is assertion**. So the deck stops pretending it is silent. The verse beat's rendered `source` line now says what the āyah is about — *"the Name's own verb, spoken of creation itself"* — which is the smallest honest thing that can be put on the screen: it neither hides the join nor extends it into a promise about the reader's loss.

**Three things bear on it:**

1. **The app's own catalogue already makes exactly this join, and cites exactly this āyah.** Catalog id 68's `hadith` field reads: *"Allah says: 'He begins creation, then He will repeat it — and that is easier for Him.' Al-Muid restores what was taken, and can replace it with better. (Quran 30:27)"*. **A user who meets this deck and then the Name card meets the same āyah twice, with the same reading, and has no contradiction to resolve.** Only `al-haleem@1` in batch 1 could say that; the other four batch-1 decks had to flag a contradiction with their own card. **That is the single strongest argument for signing this deck**, and it is an argument from the app's existing content rather than from my judgement.
2. **The bridge from `يُعِيدُهُۥ` to "what you lost can come back" is the classical reading of the Name**, not an invention — but this deck does not cite a scholar for it, because the tiering rule in §G2 makes Yaqeen-class writing a framing authority only, and framing is precisely what is at issue here. **So the deck states the smallest version of the join on the beat and goes no further.** The verse beat's `source` names the āyah's subject (creation); beat 6's `primary` quotes the āyah and stops; beat 8 talks only about the narration and never mentions creation, restoration or the reader.
3. **If the founder's bar is that the verse beat must demonstrate the same act the story demonstrates**, this deck fails and should be cut. That is a clean outcome. **Al-Muhyi (catalog id 69) is the ready replacement** — its root is in 30:50 and 75:40, its catalogue duʿā (*"revive the hopes that this world has killed"*) is the most ICP-resonant string in the whole catalogue, and this batch deliberately left both āyāt free for it (see `al-qadir@1`'s authoring notes).

### ⚠️ Reverence risk, faced — the promise this deck must not make

**Umm Salama's replacement was the Prophet ﷺ.** A deck that ends on *"and look what she got"* is telling the reader that a better thing is coming, which is (a) not a claim any source makes about the reader and (b) exactly the kind of Name-action attribution the format spec's reverence rule exists to stop.

**So beat 8 does not go near it.** It stays on what the narration records about **her**, not about what will happen to **the user**: the objection was voiced, and the sentence was said anyway. The catalogue's own `lesson` line for id 68 — *"What was taken from you — Al-Muid can restore it, or replace it with better"* — is a promise-shaped sentence about the reader, and it was deliberately **not** used as the takeaway, on the same reasoning `al-haleem@1` used for id 29's `lesson`.
