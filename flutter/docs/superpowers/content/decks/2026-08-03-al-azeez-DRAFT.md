## 1 · The enumeration that decides this deck

Run over the **full 6,236-āyah Uthmānī text** (`api.quran.com/api/v4/quran/verses/uthmani`,
6,236 verses confirmed), mark-folded, **enumerated by verb form** — not by an API search
(§9ac / §9af).

`ع+ز` returns **33 distinct orthographic forms / 140 occurrences**. **17 forms (21 occurrences)
belong to other roots** and were removed by eye, not by the screen: `ʿ-z-m` ×6 (`عزم عزموا تعزموا
عزمت عزما العزم`), `ʿ-z-r` ×3 (`وعزرتموهم وعزروه وتعزروه`), `ʿ-z-l` ×3 (`معزل لمعزولون عزلت`),
`ʿ-z-b` ×1 (`يعزب`), `m-ʿ-z` ×1 (`المعز`, *goats*), the proper nouns `عزير` and `والعزى`, and
`عزين`.

**The remaining 16 forms / 119 occurrences are `ʿ-z-z`.** They classify as follows:

| class | citations / forms | occurrences | bar-1 verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Allah is the subject of a `ʿ-z-z` verb** | `وَتُعِزُّ` (3:26), `فَعَزَّزْنَا` (36:14) | **2** | **the only bar-1-eligible texts in the Qurʾān** |
| a **human** is the subject of a `ʿ-z-z` verb | `وَعَزَّنِى فِى ٱلْخِطَابِ` (38:23) | 1 | not Allah's act |
| definite epithet `ٱلْعَزِيزُ` | — | 64, **of which 4 are the Egyptian minister** (12:30, 12:51, 12:78, 12:88) | the other 60 are trailing/appositive epithets — **bar 1 forbids** |
| indefinite `عَزِيزٌ` / `عَزِيزًا` | — | 24 + 7 | same |
| `بِعَزِيزٍ` | 11:91 (a human addressing Shuʿayb); 14:20, 35:17, 39:37 (`وَمَا ذَٰلِكَ عَلَى ٱللَّهِ بِعَزِيزٍ` — *"that is not difficult for Allah"*) | 4 | a **different sense** — not-hard, not might |
| `ٱلْعِزَّة` as a noun | 2:206 (**human arrogance**), 4:139 ×2, 10:65, 35:10 ×2, 37:180, 63:8 | 8 | the divine ones **state**, they do not show (bar 2) |
| `ʿizza` predicated of a human, an idol, or Pharaoh | 5:54, 27:34, 11:92, 18:34, 19:81, 26:44 (`بِعِزَّةِ فِرْعَوْنَ`), 38:2, 38:82 (Iblīs's oath), 63:8 (`ٱلْأَعَزُّ`) | 9 | not Allah's act |

**Restated as the one sentence that matters: the Qurʾān contains exactly three finite `ʿ-z-z` verbs.
One has a human subject (38:23). The other two are 3:26 and 36:14.**

**3:26 `وَتُعِزُّ مَن تَشَآءُ` is spent** — shipped `al-malik@1` renders 3:26 on its verse beat, and
3:26 **is** catalogue id 4's duʿā, so it cannot move (ledger §9ai).

**Therefore 36:14 `فَعَزَّزْنَا بِثَالِثٍ` is the only unspent text in the Qurʾān that meets bar 1
for this Name.** The deck is built on it and the trade-off table above is why.

**Limit of this enumeration, stated:** it enumerates the **root** `ʿ-z-z` across the Uthmānī rasm.
It does not enumerate synonyms of might (`q-w-y`, `gh-l-b`, `q-h-r`, `j-b-r`), which belong to other
Names, several already decked.

## 4 · The five bars

**Bar 1 — demonstrated in the cited text, in Allah's words, not a trailing epithet. MET, in the
strongest form available for this Name.** 36:14's `فَعَزَّزْنَا` is a **finite verb of the Name's own
root with Allah as the first-person subject**, inside the clause that performs the act. It is
neither a trailing epithet nor the deck's prose. **Measurement:** of 119 `ʿ-z-z` occurrences, **3** are
finite verbs, **2** of those have Allah as subject, and **1 of the 2 is spent** (§1).

**Bar 2 — shown, not stated. MET on the story; the verse beat states, by design.** The story does
not say Allah is mighty; it narrates two messengers being disbelieved and a third being sent.
The verse beat (10:65) does state — that is the shipped norm for verse beats
(`al-lateef@1`'s 42:19 *"Allah is Ever Kind to His servants"*), and the deck's distinguishing
quality is carried by the story, matching `al-kareem@1`, whose bar 1 sits on its ḥadīth and whose
verse beat is Sulaymān's speech.

## 6 · Bar 5 — the argument, stated so it can be attacked

**36:13–14's own passage ends at 36:29 with the town destroyed by a single shout.** That is the row
to attack first, and I am not going to soften it.

> **CORRECTION (post-verification, 2026-08-03).** This section originally argued only from 36:29
> (15 āyāt out). The wave-2 adversarial verifier fetched 36:16–19 and found **36:18** — an explicit
> threat of stoning and *"painful punishment"* against the messengers — only **4 āyāt** past the
> deck's last quoted material (36:14), closer and in a harsher register than 36:29. It was missing
> from the original disclosure entirely; the sweep below is extended to cover it, argued on the same
> terms already used for 36:29. **General lesson, recorded because this is the same shape as a
> wave-1 deck that disclosed a distant successor and missed the adjacent one: when disclosing a
> bar-5 successor, walk outward from n+1 — do not stop at the first thing that looks quotable.**

The case that it does not fail bar 5, in order:

1. **The sweep is clean at n±1, but not further out — corrected.** 36:12 is life-giving; 36:15 is
   the town's denial with no punishment in it. **36:16–17 are the messengers' reply** (`قَالُوا۟
   رَبُّنَا يَعْلَمُ إِنَّآ إِلَيْكُمْ لَمُرْسَلُونَ`, "our Lord knows that we are messengers to
   you," plus "our duty is only to deliver the message plainly") — clean, no punishment. **36:18 is
   the town's threat**, only 4 āyāt out: *"They said, 'Indeed, we consider you a bad omen. If you do
   not desist, we will surely stone you, and you will surely be touched by a painful punishment from
   us.'"* **36:19** is the messengers' answer, rejecting the omen-claim and warning the town is
   "transgressing." The nearest **narrated, carried-out** punishment is still **fifteen āyāt** past
   the last story beat, at 36:29 — 36:18 is a threat, spoken by the town, never enacted on-screen.
2. **Calibration against what already ships.** Shipped `al-afuw@1`'s verse beat has
   *"the disbelievers will have a severe punishment"* at **n+1** — divine, eschatological, and
   aimed at a class the reader might fear belonging to — and the ledger records it non-blocking
   because it contradicts no beat. My nearest *enacted* punishment (36:29) is fifteen āyāt out,
   temporal, and aimed at the antagonists — softer on every axis. My nearest *threatened* punishment
   (36:18) is only 4 āyāt out, but it is **spoken by the antagonists, about the messengers, and never
   carried out** — a materially different shape from `al-afuw@1`'s narrated eschatological outcome.
   **A rule cannot forbid the softer case while shipping the harder one** (the §9aa calibration,
   applied to both 36:18 and 36:29).
3. **Question 3 of the sweep — does the excerpt stop short of the passage's ending in a way that
   changes its meaning?** The deck's claim is *"the disbelieved were reinforced, and the town never
   agreed."* Neither 36:18 nor 36:29 contradicts that — 36:18 is the town escalating to a threat
   (still refusing to agree), and 36:29 confirms the town never agreed. What either would contradict
   is a claim of eventual vindication — **and the deck makes the opposite claim, on beat 8, in the
   text the user reads.**
4. **The register risk is real and separately handled — and 36:18 is exactly the risk named.**
   The founder's warning for this Name is *do not conflate might with victory in battle*. **No beat
   renders fighting, an enemy, a defeat, a shout, an extinction, a stoning-threat, or a consequence
   of any kind.** The measurement: `battle`, `enemy`, `victory`, `destroyed`, `shout`, `stone*`,
   `punish*` appear in **zero** of this deck's eight rendered strings. The whole deck is two
   messengers, a third messenger, and a town that says no — **36:18's stoning threat sits one tap
   past that "no," and is the nearest thing to the founder's named danger of anything fetched for
   this deck.** It is disclosed here for exactly that reason.

**What a verifier should press on:** whether a deck may draw comfort from the middle of a passage
whose next-quoted-in-full-would-be verse is a stoning threat (36:18) and whose end is a nation's
destruction (36:29), when the deck's user is being told *"the room will not agree with you, and that
is survivable."* I think yes, and I think the deck's own beat 8 is what makes it yes. That is an
argument, not a 404. Rule against it if you disagree.

## 9 · What I could not verify — limits of this method

1. **No corpus independent of quran.com.** Every āyah here was fetched from `api.quran.com/api/v4`.
   The Uthmānī text used for the root enumeration came from the **same** API
   (`/quran/verses/uthmani`), so the enumeration and the citations are **not independent of each
   other**. A muṣḥaf-independent re-run is the strongest single check a verifier could make on §1.
2. **No isnād was audited.** This deck cites no ḥadīth on any beat, so no published grade line is
   load-bearing — but the rejections in §8 that name ḥadīth were read from sunnah.com pages via
   Wayback, with the same limitation.
3. **Morphology is author-assigned.** `فَعَزَّزْنَا` as Form II and `وَتُعِزُّ` as Form IV were read
   by eye off the fetched Arabic. **No morphological database (Quranic Arabic Corpus, Shamela,
   Dorar) was consulted.** The mechanical screen produces false positives — **17 of the 33 `ع+ز`
   forms are other roots** — and I removed those by eye, which is exactly where an error would hide.
   **An earlier version of §1 in this file said "fifteen belong to other roots" and "the 18 that are
   `ʿ-z-z`". Both were wrong; the true figures are 17 and 16. They were caught by re-running the
   count against the table instead of against my own prose (§9aj), before this draft was reported.**
4. **The duʿā's provenance is "not found", not "does not exist."** See §3.
5. **`dua_translation` was not audited against `dua_arabic` for id 8** beyond noting they
   correspond; ledger §9m/§9t record a ~30% defect rate in that column across an unselected sample
   of ten. For id 8 the Arabic `يَا عَزِيزُ أَعِزَّنِي بِطَاعَتِكَ` and the English *"O Almighty,
   honor me through obedience to You"* **do** correspond clause for clause — checked, and reported
   as a check rather than an assumption.
6. **Whether shipped `al-khaliq@1`'s 36:36 survives its own successor sweep** is not re-verified
   here; my Yā Sīn crowding disclosure assumes only that it renders 36:36, which I read from the
   asset.
