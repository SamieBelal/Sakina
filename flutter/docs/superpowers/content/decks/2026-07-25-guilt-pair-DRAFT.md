## Deck `al-ghaffar@1` — Name₁

**bridge:** For the weight you named — the sin you keep returning from — there is a Name that keeps answering.
**name_intro** *(catalog id 11, verbatim)*: الْغَفَّارُ — Al-Ghaffar — The Ever-Forgiving
**story — The servant who kept returning (Sahih al-Bukhari 7507):**
> 1. A man sinned, and said: my Lord, I have sinned — forgive me. And he was forgiven. Time passed, and he fell again.
> 2. Again he asked. Again he was forgiven. And a third time — Allah answered that His servant knew he had a Lord who forgives, and He forgave him.
> 3. In the story, the loop you are ashamed of ends the same way every time — not at the sin, but at the return.
**verse:** "O My servants who have exceeded the limits against their souls! Do not lose hope in Allah's mercy, for Allah certainly forgives all sins." — Qur'an 39:53 (excerpt)
**dua** *(catalog 11, verbatim)*: رَبِّ اغْفِرْ لِي وَتُبْ عَلَيَّ إِنَّكَ أَنْتَ التَّوَّابُ الْغَفُورُ · *Rabbighfir li wa tub 'alayya innaka Antat-Tawwabul-Ghafur* · "My Lord, forgive me and accept my repentance. You are the Acceptor of Repentance, the Forgiving."
**takeaway (pair synergy):** Al-Ghaffar is the forgiving that does not run out. At-Tawwab — the second Name of your answer — is about the turning back itself.

**Sources:** [Bukhari 7507](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:7507) (sahih, verified via archive; beat 2 is marked paraphrase — the hadith's full clause is "a Lord who forgives sins and punishes for them"; scholars read the ending as "so long as he keeps repenting" — beat 3 keeps the return in view) · [Qur'an 39:53](https://quran.com/39/53) ✅

## Deck `at-tawwab@1` — Name₂

**bridge:** You think the shameful part is having to come back again. Coming back has its own Name.
**name_intro** *(catalog id 31, verbatim)*: التَّوَّابُ — At-Tawwab — The Acceptor of Repentance
**story — The man who took a hundred lives (Sahih al-Bukhari 3470):**
> 1. A man of an earlier nation had taken ninety-nine lives. He asked: is repentance possible for me? He was told no — and the count became one hundred. Still, he kept asking.
> 2. Then he was told: go to a righteous town. He set out — and died on the road, still on his way.
> 3. The angels of mercy and the angels of punishment disputed over him. And the earth was commanded to draw the town closer — he was found a single span nearer to it, and forgiven.
**verse:** "Then Adam was inspired with words ˹of prayer˺ by His Lord, so He accepted his repentance. Surely He is the Accepter of Repentance, Most Merciful." — Qur'an 2:37 (Name in-text)
**dua** *(catalog 31, verbatim)*: اللَّهُمَّ اغْفِرْ لِي وَتُبْ عَلَيَّ إِنَّكَ أَنْتَ التَّوَّابُ الرَّحِيمُ · *Allahumma ighfir li wa tub alayyah innaka anta at-tawwabur-rahim* · "O Allah, forgive me and accept my repentance. Indeed You are At-Tawwab, the Most Merciful."
**takeaway:** In every one of these stories, the acceptance met the person mid-road. The turning is enough to begin.

**Sources:** [Bukhari 3470](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3470) (sahih, verified via archive — story copy uses ONLY Bukhari 3470 details; the "scholar not monk / leave the land" details are Muslim 2766, UNVERIFIED, excluded) · [Qur'an 2:37](https://quran.com/2/37) ✅
