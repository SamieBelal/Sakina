## Deck `al-jabbar@1` — Name₁

**bridge:** For the weight you named — the heaviness that doesn't lift — there is a Name for what is broken being mended.
**name_intro** *(catalog id 9, verbatim)*: الْجَبَّارُ — Al-Jabbar — The Compeller — Restorer of the Broken
*✅ DECIDED (founder, 2026-07-25): dual rendering adopted — catalog `english` amended to "The Compeller — Restorer of the Broken" (json + code render-sites updated); the title now matches the story and the dua.*
**story — Yaqub's grief, and the mending of the house of Yusuf (Qur'an 12):**
> 1. Yaqub wept for his son Yusuf — grief so heavy it took his eyesight. He carried it to one place only: "I complain of my anguish and sorrow only to Allah."
> 2. Then came the day a shirt arrived — the same kind of shirt that once carried false blood — and this time it carried his son's scent. "He cast the shirt over Jacob's face, so he regained his sight."
> 3. Sight restored. Son restored. Whole again.
**verse** *(excerpt, marked)*: "…do not lose hope in the mercy of Allah…" — Qur'an 12:87
**dua** *(catalog 9, verbatim)*: يَا جَبَّارُ اجْبُرْ كَسْرِي · *Ya Jabbar ujbur kasri* · "O Compeller, mend my brokenness."
**takeaway (pair synergy):** Al-Jabbar is the Name for what broke. Ash-Shafi — the second Name of your answer — is the Name for what still hurts.

## Deck `ash-shafi@1` — Name₂

**bridge:** Some heaviness is a wound. This is the Name the Prophet ﷺ himself called on for healing.
**name_intro** *(catalog id 38, verbatim)*: الشَّافِي — Ash-Shafi — The Healer
**story — Ayyub: the quietest complaint, the doubled answer (Qur'an 21:83-84):**
> 1. Ayyub lost his health, then his family. His whole prayer was one line: "I have been touched with adversity, and You are the Most Merciful of the merciful."
> 2. He stated the pain and named the Mercy. He demanded nothing.
> 3. "So We answered his prayer and removed his adversity, and gave him back his family, twice as many, as a mercy from Us."
**verse:** "And He ˹alone˺ heals me when I am sick." — Qur'an 26:80
**dua** *(catalog 38, verbatim — the Prophet's ﷺ ruqyah as the catalog carries it; cf. [Sahih al-Bukhari 5743](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5743), whose wording/clause order differs slightly)*: اللَّهُمَّ رَبَّ النَّاسِ أَذْهِبِ الْبَأْسَ اشْفِ أَنتَ الشَّافِي لَا شِفَاءَ إِلَّا شِفَاؤُكَ شِفَاءً لَا يُغَادِرُ سَقَمًا · *Allahumma Rabban-nasi, adhhib il-ba's, ishfi anta'sh-Shafi, la shifa'a illa shifa'uk, shifa'an la yughadiru saqama* · "O Allah, Lord of people, remove the illness, heal — You are the Healer, there is no healing except Your healing, a healing that leaves no illness behind."
**takeaway:** The answer to Ayyub was not repair. It was more than there was before the breaking.
