## 3 · The rulings the brief asked for

### §9bk ladder — angelic speech inside divine narration does NOT carry bar 1

11:73 is `قَالُوٓا۟ أَتَعْجَبِينَ مِنْ أَمْرِ ٱللَّهِ...إِنَّهُۥ حَمِيدٌ مَّجِيدٌ`. The subject of `قَالُوٓا۟` is the messengers (traditionally understood as angels), not Allah. The words `إِنَّهُۥ حَمِيدٌ مَّجِيدٌ` are what they said, reported by Allah.

The deck's argument is that this is "nearer to Allah quoting Himself inside a narrative than to human speech about Allah," because Allah is the one choosing to report it. **That argument proves too much.** By the same logic, *any* reported speech Allah narrates — including a prophet's, which the ladder already rules does not carry — would count as "Allah quoting Himself," because Allah is always the one narrating it. That collapses exactly the distinction the ladder exists to hold: not *who chose to report the words*, but *whose words they are*. "Allah quoting Himself" has to mean the quoted content is Allah's own first-person self-reference (a hadith qudsi's "Allah said: I am..."; a Qur'anic instance where the narrated speaker is Allah). Here the narrated speaker is a created being.

Structurally, 11:73 is closer to rung 4 ("a prophet's reported speech") than to rung 2: a being other than Allah says something true and praiseworthy about Allah, and Allah's narration transmits it faithfully. Angels are not human, so rung 5's literal wording ("human speech") doesn't apply, but the ladder's own stated organizing principle — "who is speaking" — puts them with the other non-Allah speakers, not with Allah.

**Ruling: bar 1 fails for `al-majeed@1` on 11:73.** The root sweep is independently confirmed complete at four occurrences (§1 row 20): 50:1 and 85:21 predicate the Qur'an, not Allah; 85:15 is a bare epithet the deck itself already concedes "labels, does not demonstrate," and has been surrendered to `al-majid@1` regardless. **This Name has no remaining Qur'anic route to bar 1.**

The unexplored hadith route was checked this session (§1 row 31): Tirmidhi 3507's 99-Names enumeration contains `الْمَجِيدُ`, graded **Da'if (Darussalam)** on the page, and is itself a prophet's reported enumeration rather than Allah's own voice — so even if it were sound, it would sit on the same non-carrying rung, not above it. It does not rescue this deck.

**Consequence: `al-majeed@1` (id 58) should not ship in its current form. It is a genuine refusal on bar 1**, exactly as the deck's own "what I could not determine" section predicted as its most likely outcome. This is not a defect in the drafting — the sweep is honest and the argument for the rung is stated so it can be attacked, which is what happened.

### Should ids 58 and 72 be two decks?

**Keep them as two decks, but do not ship id 58 in its current form.** The two glosses ("Glorious" vs "Noble") and the two engines as drafted (majesty arriving in a household vs. nobility that doesn't diminish when spent on those who can't repay) are genuinely distinguishable, and merging them costs the catalogue's 99-name collectible mechanic a card for a reason that isn't forced by the text — the roots are the same, but the Name-*forms* are different (`ٱلْمَجِيدُ` vs `ٱلْمَاجِدُ`), which is exactly the kind of distinction classical enumerations of the divine names preserve rather than collapse (Tirmidhi 3507 itself lists both, back to back, as separate names — §1 row 31).

The cost of keeping them separate is that, as things stand, one of the two (id 58) has no textual route to a ship-ready deck: its whole root is spent at four occurrences, two are the Qur'an-as-subject, one is a labeling epithet already given away, and the fourth (11:73) fails bar 1 on the ruling above. That is an honest gap in the catalogue, not a reason to merge two Names that mean different things. The cost of merging is worse: it resolves a textual shortfall by an editorial decision the text doesn't support, and it takes a card off the board in a product where completing the set is the point.

### 39:67 vs. `al-malik@1`'s hadith — should Qur'an outrank a shipped hadith rendering?

**No, not automatically, and not in this specific case — the current choice (56:68–74) is correct on its own merits, independent of the al-malik@1 consideration.** Two independent reasons converge:

1. **39:67 needs its own trade.** Confirmed by fetch (§1 row 7): the root `ع-ظ-م` does not appear in 39:67 at all. Using it would cost this deck a bar-4 trade it does not currently need — 56:74 carries the root with zero trade.
2. **39:67 would collide with `al-malik@1` on imagery, not just on a shared root.** Confirmed by fetch (§1 row 30): Sahih al-Bukhari 4812's own chapter heading is explicitly keyed to 39:67 ("The Statement of Allah the Most High: '...the whole earth will be grasped by His Hand and the heavens will be rolled up in His Right Hand...' (V.39:67)"). This is not a coincidental echo the drafter is being cautious about — the hadith collection itself frames that scene as 39:67's illustration. Rendering 39:67 on `al-azeem@1` would put the same picture (grip, right hand, earth, heavens) on two decks regardless of which testament each text comes from, which is exactly what bar 3's sibling-collapse rule exists to catch.

Given 56:68–74 already clears all five bars with no trade anywhere and is, on the deck's own accounting, "the tightest carrier/verse relationship in this wave" (56:74 is the passage's own conclusion), there is no case here for reopening `al-malik@1`. The general question — should a Qur'anic text ever outrank a shipped hadith rendering of the same scene — remains open in the abstract, but this instance does not present it: the Qur'anic alternative is strictly worse on textual grounds before the collision is even considered.
